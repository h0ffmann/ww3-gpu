# THEME

Third-generation spectral wave models solve the wave action balance equation on a spatial grid and a spectral grid of frequencies and directions, integrating wind input, nonlinear interactions between components and dissipation at every point and every time step, with no *ad hoc* assumption about the shape of the spectrum [@wamdi1988; @tolman1991]. WAVEWATCH III® (WW3), developed at NOAA/NCEP, is the reference model of this class, maintained by NOAA/NCEP and distributed as open source for operational and research use [@ww3manual; @ww3repo]. It is written in Fortran, parallelised with MPI and OpenMP, and a *switch file* fixes at compile time which parts of the code enter the executable: the physics, the propagation scheme and the I/O options [@ww3manual].

Wave forecasting inherits the nonlinearity of the atmospheric forecast that drives it, and its skill degrades with lead time: the assessment of the NCEP ensemble system against four altimeter missions shows the error growing along the forecast days, with the ensemble mean cutting the scatter error of significant wave height from 1.8 m in the control run to 1.3 m [@campos2020]. The operational answer is to run more often and in ensemble, perturbing initial conditions and forcing [@farina2002]. NCEP, for instance, runs GFS-Wave four times a day (00, 06, 12 and 18 UTC) out to 384 h on global grids of 0.16° and 0.25° with regional nests, and GEFS-Wave with 30 members at 0.25° out to 16 days [@emcwaves]. Every one of those choices — cycle frequency, resolution, number of nested domains and of members — is bounded by the wall-clock time of a forecast and by the computing resources available: wall-clock time decides how much physics and how much resolution fit inside the delivery deadline.

The Laboratório de Engenharia e Ciências Oceânicas (LabECO) of the Department of Mechanical Engineering at the Universidade Federal de Santa Catarina (UFSC) runs WW3 as part of its contribution to the Rede Nacional de Observação e Monitoramento Oceânico (ReNOMO), a national network funded by the CNPq/MCTI/Finep 062/2022 call that integrates ocean observation, quality control and operational product delivery for the South Atlantic and the Brazilian coast [@renomo; @labeco]. That chain, rather than a laboratory installation, constitutes the reference case of this work.

The theme of this project is the **parallel simulation and GPU acceleration of a third-generation spectral wave model**, taking WW3 in the configuration operated by LabECO as its case. It is a performance-engineering problem with a measurable result — the wall-clock time of a forecast cycle — under the constraint that the correctness of the forecast fields is never traded for speed.
