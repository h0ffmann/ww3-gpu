# THEME

WAVEWATCH III® (WW3) is a numerical model for forecasting sea waves, maintained by the National Centers for Environmental Prediction (NCEP) of the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) and distributed as open source [@ww3manual; @ww3repo]. The model splits the ocean into a grid of points and keeps, at each point, the wave spectrum: how much energy there is in each band of frequency and direction. At every time step it carries that energy from point to point (propagation) and changes it in place through wind input, energy exchange between waves and wave breaking (source terms). This formulation, called third-generation, solves the wave action balance equation without imposing the shape of the spectrum in advance [@wamdi1988; @tolman1991]. The code is written in Fortran, parallelised with MPI and OpenMP, and a *switch file* selects at compile time which parts of the code enter the executable: the physics, the propagation scheme and the I/O options [@ww3manual]. The Laboratório de Engenharia e Ciências Oceânicas of the Universidade Federal de Santa Catarina (LabECO/UFSC) runs WW3 in its operational forecasting system, as part of the Rede Nacional de Observação e Monitoramento Oceânico (ReNOMO) [@renomo; @labeco].

The theme of this project is the **parallel simulation and GPU acceleration of a spectral wave model**, taking WW3 in LabECO's operational configuration as its case. The problem is to reduce the run time of each forecast cycle without changing the forecast fields beyond tolerances agreed with the laboratory. The working hypothesis is that this time can be reduced by a sequence of steps of increasing cost (tuning the build and the configuration, refactoring in modern Fortran, and rewriting the most expensive routines in C++/Kokkos to run on a GPU) and that each gain can be measured and validated against the current run.
